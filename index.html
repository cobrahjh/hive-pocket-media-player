    Feature reminders

    How it works
    • Microphone — in this menu. It draws from whatever the phone can hear, which is the only way to get visuals from music this app cannot read — YouTube, Spotify, another phone across the room, a radio. Play it out loud and the bars and bursts follow it.
    • Folder button — choose music from this phone. On Chrome the app remembers the folder and brings it back with one tap next time; on other browsers it asks again each time, because nothing there can remember one.
    • Link button — paste a direct address to an audio file. Saved links come back every time.
    • Tap the visuals for full screen. Tap again to come back.
    • Drag a finger across them and they paint — bursts follow your finger, in whatever effect and colours are set. A press and hold does it too. It works with no music at all, which makes it the one thing here that needs nothing but a finger.
    • Two fingers on the visuals sends a pair of wisps off — a light leaves each finger, trailing a long tail that fades away behind it, and drifts the screen on its own, throwing a different effect wherever it goes. You touch once; you do not have to hold. They keep going until you press two fingers again, which sends them away: the same gesture starts and stops them. Two at a time, and while they are out the music adds none of its own — what you sent beats what it would have thrown, so the beat stops throwing wisps of its own until you dismiss yours.
    • Star button — effects on or off. It is the same setting as On the stage above. A finger on the stage turns them back on: dragging, holding or two fingers all mean "draw something here", so the app does rather than refusing quietly. A plain tap does not — that is still full screen and nothing else.
    • Previous goes back a track, or starts the current one again if you are more than a few seconds in.
    • YouTube was removed in 1.18.0. Its sound came from another site, which a web app is not allowed to read, so it was the one thing here that played to a dead stage — no bars, no bursts. Play it out loud in the YouTube app and press Listen instead, and the visuals follow it properly. Links saved before are kept but say so when pressed.
    • Which browser matters, and where. The microphone, the visuals, the equalizer and everything a finger does use ordinary web features and work in Chrome, Firefox and Safari alike. Only remembering a music folder is Chrome-and-Chromium — nothing else can remember one — so on other browsers the folder button asks each time and everything else is identical. Safari also wants a tap before it opens a microphone, so "start listening when the app opens" may not start it there by itself.
    • Reacts to — which parts of the sound throw a burst. Bass is the kick drum; mids are voices and guitars, mid-screen; highs are hats and cymbals, near the top.
    • Lightning is in both the Bursts list and the finger setting. A bolt strikes on the beat, or down to wherever you touch. It flashes, so Random and Surprise me never choose it for you.
    • Report a problem — in this menu. Write what happened and press Send by email; your mail app opens with the report and the app's own state already in it, and you press send there. Nothing about your music is included, and you can read exactly what is attached before you send it.
    • Everything works offline now, including the next time you open the app. Nothing in this app reaches the network at all.
    • Look — one tap sets the equalizer, the burst, the colours, the background and the sensitivity together. Move any of them by hand and the picker says Custom. Storm flashes the screen; if that bothers you, or you are photosensitive, leave it alone. Surprise me rolls all five but never the two flashing backgrounds — those are yours to choose by name, not something a button should spring on you.
    • Random equalizer picks one style and holds it — it changes when you change something else, rather than flickering between seven.
    • Size scales every burst. On Battery saver the particle budget clips the biggest ones — that is the cap doing its job, not the setting failing.
    • Sensitivity — a room smears the sharp edges a file keeps, so what works for a track playing here will never fire from across the room. The cost of Easy is honesty: a burst stops meaning a distinct beat was hit.
    • Reacts to — every burst used to come from the lowest four of sixty-four bands, the kick drum and nothing else. Mids are voices, snares and guitars and land mid-screen; highs are hats and cymbals, near the top. Mids have to be proportionally dominant rather than merely loud, because a drum hit raises everything at once and would otherwise count twice.
    • Colours colour every layer at once. Random rolls the effects; the equalizer takes one real colour from the set, since it cannot roll.
    • Performance — Automatic starts from what this phone says about itself, then watches its own frame rate and moves between Full, Balanced and Battery saver. Coming down, bursts throw fewer pieces, the background carries fewer, and Battery saver redraws the equalizer 24 times a second instead of 60. The menu names the running tier and the frame rate that decided it, so a downgrade is never silent.
    • Player — Minimized keeps what you touch and drops what you only read: the transport and the track name stay, the queue and the scrubber go. Hidden leaves only the visuals and this menu; anything playing stops, since there would be no way to stop it.
    • The microphone keeps the screen awake while it listens, so a phone propped against something does not go dark mid-track. Measured: about 12% of a battery an hour, screen and all.
    • If the folder keeps asking — Chrome's prompt sometimes offers three answers, and the middle one, Allow on every visit, is the one meant to stick. Chrome documents that an installed app keeps the permission with no prompt; on a real Android phone it asks anyway, and nothing in this app can override that. A problem report carries exactly what the browser answered.
    • Saved links — any YouTube links saved by an older version are kept but cannot play; Forget all saved links removes those too.

    About

    Hive Pocket Media Player
    Everything runs on this phone. Nothing here reaches the network after it loads, and your music is never uploaded — there is nowhere for it to go. Privacy.

    On the home screen it opens without the browser bars. It does not stop the folder asking after a cold start — that is Android, not this app.

    To put this on your home screen, open your browser's own menu — the three dots in Chrome, the share button in Safari — and choose Install app or Add to Home screen. A button appears here instead once Chrome offers one.
